**An $\alpha$-particle ($^{4}_{2}\text{He}$) is travelling in a vacuum directly towards the centre of a stationary gold nucleus ($^{197}_{79}\text{Au}$), as illustrated in Fig. 1.1.**

[Insert Diagram: A small circle ($+2e$) with a velocity arrow pointing towards a larger circle ($+79e$).]

**The $\alpha$-particle is emitted from a source with an initial kinetic energy of 4.8 MeV. At a large distance from the gold nucleus, the $\alpha$-particle has its maximum speed. As it approaches the nucleus, it slows down until it comes to a momentary rest at a distance $d$ from the centre of the gold nucleus.**

**The $\alpha$-particle and the gold nucleus may be considered to be point charges that are isolated in space.**

**(a) Explain why the $\alpha$-particle comes to rest at a distance $d$ from the gold nucleus. [2]**

**(b) Calculate, for the $\alpha$-particle:**
   **1. its initial kinetic energy, in J, [2]**

### **Key "Cambridge Style" Changes Made:**
1.  **Removed "Fired from a gun":** Particles are "emitted from a source" or "incident on" a target.
2.  **Replaced "Infinity" with "Large distance":** Standard terminology to describe the point where potential energy is negligible.
3.  **Nuclear Notation:** Explicitly provided $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ and $^{197}_{79}\text{Au}$ so the student knows to use $Q = 79e$ and $q = 2e$.
4.  **Step-by-step Calculation:** Part (b) forces the conversion to Joules first. This is a common "trap" or structured step in CIE papers.
5.  **Qualitative Part:** Added part (a) using the command word **"Explain,"** which is a hallmark of Paper 4 theory questions.
