### 3. Attenuation of X-rays in matter ($I = I_0e^{-\mu x}$)
*Recall and use $I = I_0e^{-\mu x}$ for the attenuation of X-rays in matter.*

> [!question] October/November 2025 Paper 42 Question 10(b)
> X-rays of intensity $I_0$ are incident normally on a structure. Material P has a linear attenuation coefficient of $0.35 \text{ cm}^{-1}$ and thickness 5.8 cm. Material Q is embedded inside P with thickness 2.1 cm. 
> The X-rays emerging from the structure in region A (passing through P only) have an intensity of $0.053I_0$.
> (i) Show that the intensity of the X-rays emerging in region B (passing through P only) is $0.13I_0$. [1]
> (ii) Determine the linear attenuation coefficient $\mu$ of material Q. [3]
> 
> > [!success] Mark Scheme
> > **(i)**
> > * $I = I_0 \exp(-\mu x) = I_0 \exp(-5.8 \times 0.35) = 0.13 I_0$ [A1]
> > 
> > **(ii)**
> > * use of $\exp\{-(0.35 \times 3.7)\}$ factor [C1]
> > * $0.053I_0 = I_0 \exp\{-[(0.35 \times 3.7) + 2.1\mu]\}$ [C1]
> > * $\mu = 0.78 \text{ cm}^{-1}$ [A1]

> [!question] February/March 2024 Paper 42 Question 9(b)
> An X-ray beam is incident normally on a sample of soft tissue and bone. The total thickness of soft tissue is $x$. The total thickness of bone is also $x$. The linear attenuation coefficient for soft tissue is $0.22 \text{ cm}^{-1}$ and for bone is $3.0 \text{ cm}^{-1}$.
> The incident intensity of the X-ray beam is $I_0$. The transmitted intensity of the X-ray beam is 13% of the incident intensity. Determine $x$, in cm. [3]
> 
> > [!success] Mark Scheme
> > * $I = I_0 \exp(-\mu t)$ [C1]
> > * $0.13 = [\exp(-3.0x)] \times [\exp(-0.22x)] = \exp(-3.22x)$ [C1]
> > * $x = 0.63 \text{ cm}$ [A1]

> [!question] October/November 2023 Paper 41 Question 10(b)
> For one type of wave passing through tissue, the wave has 72% of its initial intensity after it has passed through 6.2 cm of the tissue. 
> (i) Calculate the linear attenuation coefficient $\mu$ of the tissue for this wave. [2]

> 
> > [!success] Mark Scheme

> [!question] May/June 2023 Paper 42 Question 10(b)

> > * $I = I_0 \exp(-\mu x)$ [C1]

> > * $\%$ absorbed $= (1.000 - 0.0198) \times 100 = 98\%$ [A1]
