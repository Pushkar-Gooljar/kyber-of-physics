# 🧲 Chapter 20: Magnetic Fields - Master Cheatsheet

> [!abstract] Overview
> This chapter covers the interaction between magnetic fields, electric currents, and moving charges, as well as the phenomenon of electromagnetic induction.

---

## 1. Concept of a Magnetic Field

*   **Definition:** A region of space where a magnetic material (magnetic pole) or a moving charge experiences a force.
*   **Representing Fields:**
    *   Magnetic field lines are continuous loops.
    *   Direction: North to South (outside the magnet).
    *   Density of lines = Magnitude of Flux Density ($B$).
    *   **3D Representation:**
        *   `●` (Dot): Out of the page.
        *   `×` (Cross): Into the page.

## 2. Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor

*   **Equation:** $F = BIL \sin \theta$
    *   $F$ = Magnetic force (N)
    *   $B$ = Magnetic flux density (T)
    *   $I$ = Current (A)
    *   $L$ = Length of conductor *within the field* (m)
    *   $\theta$ = Angle between the current direction and the magnetic field lines.
*   **Direction (Fleming's Left-Hand Rule):**
    *   **Thumb:** Motion/Force ($F$)
    *   **First Finger:** Field ($B$)
    *   **Second Finger:** Current ($I$) (Conventional: + to -)

![Fleming's Left-Hand Rule](https://cognito.org/courses/alevel/physics/cie/notes/cnBOcEp4U1I/11.2-fleming-s-left-hand-rule)

> [!warning] Common Pitfalls
> *   $F, B,$ and $I$ must be mutually perpendicular for $F=BIL$ to hold at maximum.
> *   If the current is parallel to field lines, $\theta = 0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$, so $\sin\theta = 0$, $F = 0$.
> *   Remember: Direction of current for negative charges (electrons) is opposite to their motion.

---

## 3. Magnetic Flux Density ($B$)

*   **Definition:** The force per unit length per unit current on a straight wire placed at right angles to the magnetic field.
    *   Unit: **Tesla (T)**.
    *   1 Tesla = 1 Newton per Ampere-metre.

---

## 4. Forces on a Moving Charge

*   **Equation:** $F = BQv \sin \theta$
    *   $Q$ = Charge (C)
    *   $v$ = Velocity (m/s)
*   **Circular Motion:** When $v$ is perpendicular to $B$, the magnetic force acts as a centripetal force:
    *   $BQv = \frac{mv^2}{r}$
    *   Radius of path $r = \frac{mv}{BQ}$
*   **Velocity Selector:** Used to filter particles with a specific velocity.
    *   Electric force ($F_E = EQ$) equals Magnetic force ($F_B = BQv$).
    *   $EQ = BQv \implies v = \frac{E}{B}$

![Moving charges](https://cognito.org/courses/alevel/physics/cie/notes/VXhERUNoeXQ/11.3-moving-charges-in-a-magnetic-field)

## 5. Hall Effect

*   **Concept:** When a conductor carries current in a magnetic field, charges are pushed to one side, creating a potential difference (Hall Voltage, $V_H$).
*   **Equation:** $V_H = \frac{BI}{ntq}$
    *   $n$ = number density of charge carriers ($m^{-3}$)
    *   $t$ = thickness of the slice
    *   $q$ = charge of carrier
*   **Application:** Used to measure magnetic flux density $B$ using a Hall probe.

> [!tip] Hall Probe Tip
> Smaller $n$ (semiconductors) gives a larger $V_H$, making them better for sensitive measurements than metals like copper.

---

## 6. Electromagnetic Induction

### Magnetic Flux ($\Phi$)
*   **Definition:** Product of magnetic flux density and the cross-sectional area perpendicular to the field.
*   **Formula:** $\Phi = BA \cos \theta$
    *   $\theta$ is the angle between the **field lines** and the **normal** to the plane of the area.

### Magnetic Flux Linkage
*   **Formula:** $N\Phi = BAN \cos \theta$ (for $N$ turns)

### Faraday's & Lenz's Laws
*   **Faraday's Law:** The magnitude of induced e.m.f. is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.
    *   $\varepsilon = N \frac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$
*   **Lenz's Law:** The direction of induced e.m.f. is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused it.
    *   $\varepsilon = -N \frac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$ (The negative sign represents Lenz's Law).

## 7. Examiner Guidance & Common Mistakes

> [!danger] Exam Strategy
> *   **Defining Flux Density:** Don't be ambiguous about what is perpendicular. Explicitly state: "The force per unit current, per unit length on a wire *placed at right angles* to the magnetic field."
> *   **Lenz's Law:** If asked to explain the direction, do not just define the law. Apply it: "The induced current creates a field that *opposes* the change (e.g., magnet approaching)."
> *   **Units:** Always check units! $mT \rightarrow T$ ($\times 10^{-3}$), $cm^2 \rightarrow m^2$ ($\times 10^{-4}$), $ms \rightarrow s$ ($\times 10^{-3}$).
> *   **Graphs:** If sketching induced e.m.f. from a changing flux graph, remember $\varepsilon$ is proportional to the *gradient* of the flux graph. If the flux graph has a constant positive gradient, the e.m.f. is a constant negative value.
> *   **Formula confusion:** $F=BIL$ is for wires. $F=BQv$ is for isolated charges. Don't mix them up.
> *   **Hall Effect:** When describing it, mention that the electric field set up by charge separation balances the magnetic force on the charge carriers.
