>[!info] Definition: Intensity Reflection Coefficient ($\alpha$)
>- Ratio of the intensity of the reflected wave to the intensity of the incident wave.

>[!math]
>The Intensity Reflection Coefficient can be calculated using the fraction:
>$$\alpha=\frac{I_{R}}{I_{0}}=\frac{(Z_{2}-Z_{1})^2}{(Z_{2}+Z_{1})^2}$$
>- Where:
>	* $\alpha$ = intensity reflection coefficient
>	* $I_R$ = intensity of the reflected wave ($\text{W m}^{-2}$)
>	* $I_0$ = intensity of the incident wave ($\text{W m}^{-2}$)
>	* $Z_1$ = acoustic impedance of one material ($\text{kg m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$)
>	* $Z_2$ = acoustic impedance of a second material ($\text{kg m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$)

>[!concept]
>This ratio shows that:
>1. If there is a large difference between the impedance of the two materials, then most of the energy will be reflected.
>2. If the impedance is the same, then there will be no reflection.


>[!tip]
>**The ratio of transmitted wave intensity to incident wave intensity is given by**
>$$\frac{I_{T}}{I_{0}}=1-\frac{I_{R}}{I_{0}}$$

>[!important]
>![Effect using a coupling gel|500](coupling_gel.svg)
>- The specific acoustic impedances of air and skin are very different. 
>- The intensity reflection coefficient is high and most of the ultrasound waves would be reflected at the skin.
>- To minimise reflection and ensure most of the waves enter the body, a coupling gel is used.
>- The coupling gel has a similar specific acoustic impedance to the skin, leading to a small intensity reflection coefficient.
