- The specific acoustic impedance of a medium is the product of the density of the medium and the speed of ultrasound in the medium.
- The specific acoustic impedance of a medium quantifies how much resistance an ultrasound wave encounters as it passes through the medium.
- Specific acoustic impedance can be calculated using the equation:
$$
Z=\rho c
$$
- **Where:**
	- $Z$ = specific acoustic impedance ($\text{kg}\text{ m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$)
	- $\rho$ = density of the medium ($\text{kg}\text{ m}^{-3}$)
	- $c$ = speed of sound in the medium ($\text{m}\text{ s}^{-1}$)
- The **higher** the density of a tissue, the **greater** the specific acoustic impedance
- The **faster** ultrasound waves travel through the material, the **greater** the acoustic impedance.
- At the boundary between media of different specific acoustic impedances, some of the wave is **reflected** and some is **transmitted**.
- The greater the **difference** in acoustic impedance between the two media, the greater the reflection and the smaller the transmission.
	- Two materials with the same acoustic impedance would give no reflection.
	- Two materials with a large difference in specific acoustic difference would give much larger reflections 
- Air has an acoustic impedance of $400 \text{ kg}\text{ m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$
- Skin has an acoustic impedance of $1.7 \times 10^6\text{ kg}\text{ m}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$
	- The large difference means ultrasound would be significantly reflected, hence a coupling gel is necessary.
	- the coupling gel used has a similar specific acoustic impedance to the skin, meaning that very little ultrasound is reflected
