# X-rays
>[!info] X-rays
>X-rays are electromagnetic waves which have wavelengths in the range $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-13}$ $\text{m}$

# Producing X-rays
![X-ray tube setup|500](x-ray-tube.png)

>[!concept] Generation of X-rays in an X-ray tube
>- Electrons are emitted from the heated cathode by thermionic emission.
>- The electrons are accelerated towards the anode by a large potential difference.
>- Electrons bombard the metal target and decelerate.
>- Some of their kinetic energy is converted into energy of X-ray photons.

# Minimum Wavelength 
>[!concept] Maximum Energy Gained by Electrons 
>- When an electron is accelerated across a potential difference $V$ it gains kinetic energy equal to:
>$$E_{K}=eV$$

>[!concept] Minimum Wavelength of X-ray Photons
>- Since some of the kinetic energy of the electrons are converted into X-ray photons, the maximum energy an X-ray photon can have is equal to the maximum kinetic energy of the electrons:
>$$\frac{hc}{\lambda}=eV$$
>$$\lambda=\frac{hc}{eV}$$

# X-ray Spectrum
>[!info] X-ray Emission Spectrum
>![X-ray spectrum|500](x-ray-spectrum.png)

>[!concept] Continuous Distribution
>- An X-ray photon is produced when an electron is decelerated.
>- Larger acceleration results in larger photon energy.
>- Photon energy is related to wavelength
>- Electrons hitting the metal target have a continuous range of accelerations.
>- Therefore they produce a continuous spectrum of wavelengths.


>[!concept] Sharp cut-off at short wavelength
>- Electron gives all its energy to one photon and is stopped in a single collision

>[!concept] Superimposed Peaks on the Continuous Distribution of Wavelengths
>- De-excitation of orbital electrons in the metal target emit photons whose energy is equal to the difference in energy levels.
