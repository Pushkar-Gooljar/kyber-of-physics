# W22-25

>[!info] How are gamma photons formed inside the body during PET scanning
>- A positron collides with an electron in the body producing 2 gamma photons
>- annihilation results in their masses becoming photon energy

>[!info] Definition of tracer
>- A radioactive substance introduced in the body 
>- which is absorbed by the tissues being studied

>[!info] Definition of contrast in an X-ray image
>Difference in degrees of blackening

>[!info] How X-rays are used in computed tomography (CT) scanning to produce a 3D image of an internal structure
>
>- The structure is scanned in thin sections
>- Many scans of each section is taken from different angles
>- Scanning is repeated for all sections and data is compiled by a computer to form a 3D image

>[!info] Definition of specific acoustic impedance 
>Product of density of the medium and speed of ultrasound in the medium

>[!info] How ultrasound waves are detected by a piezoelectric crystal 
>- Ultrasound waves cause crystal to vibrate
>- Vibrations of crystal cause an e.m.f. to be induced across the crystal

>[!info] How a piezoelectric crystal generates ultrasound waves
>- An alternating p.d. is applied to the crystal causing it to vibrate
>- When frequency of applied p.d. equals the natural frequency of the crystal, the crystal resonates
>- Natural frequency of the crystal is in the ultrasound range.

>[!info] How X-rays waves may be used to form images of internal body structures 
>- Waves are passed into a structure and transmitted waves are detected.
>- Different parts of the structure absorb different fractions of energy.
>- Difference in transmitted intensities are used to form an image.

>[!info] How is a piezoelectric crystal made to vibrate
>- An alternating p.d. is applied across the crystal, causing it to distort

>[!info] How reflected ultrasound pulses may be used to obtain diagnostic information about internal structures (4)
>- Time gives information about depth of boundary
>- Intensity gives information about **nature** of **boundary**


>[!info] Why is the metal target bombarded by electrons to produce X-rays made of tungsten rather than copper
>- Some kinetic energy of electrons is converted to thermal energy at target
>- Tungsten has a higher melting point and therefore does not melt easily 

>[!info] How the detection of gamma-ray photons during PET scanning is used to produce an image of the tissue being examined
>- Arrival times of photons are processed
>- Image built up of tracer concentrations in the tissue

>[!info] How X-rays are produced by electrons bombardment of a metal target
>- Electrons are accelerated by applying a high p.d.
>- Electrons hit a metal target
>- Electrons are decelerated on impact with the target
>- Kinetic energy lost by electrons is emitted as X-ray photons

>[!info] Why is gel applied to the skin during ultrasound scanning
>- Without gel, almost all of the incident ultrasound is reflected form the skin.
>- With gel, almost all of the incident ultrasound is transmitted into the body.

>[!info] How positrons come to be present in the body during PET scanning
>- A tracer containing a $\beta^+$ emitter is introduced into the body

>[!warning] An alternating voltage is applied to a piezoelectric crystal in air. What happens to the air surrounding the crystal
>- Alternating p.d. makes the crystal vibrate
>- Vibration of crystals causes air to vibrate
>- frequency is in ultrasound range
>  
> **VERY IMPORTANT:** For most of the time, ultrasound is the range since its piezo, but if you have the frequency of the AC used to cause vibrations ALWAYS check 



>[!info] How the intensity reflection coefficient ($\alpha$) depends on specific acoustic impedances ($Z_1$ and $Z_2$)
>- $\alpha$ is close to 0 when $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ are equal
>- $\alpha$ is close to 1 when $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ are very different

>[!info] Why ultrasound cannot be used to produce an image inside an air-filled cavity (e.g., lungs)
>*From 9702_m23_qp_42 (Page 26)*
>- $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ (specific acoustic impedances) are very different OR intensity reflection coefficient $\approx$ 1
>- Almost no ultrasound is transmitted into the air-filled cavity

>[!info] Reasons why transmitted ultrasound intensity through a sample may be less than calculated/expected
>- There is more than one boundary so more reflections occur
>- Some ultrasound is attenuated (absorbed) in matter


>[!info] Comparison of the detected wave used to form the image 
>- **Ultrasound:** the detected wave is **reflected**
>- **X-rays:** the detected wave is **transmitted**
