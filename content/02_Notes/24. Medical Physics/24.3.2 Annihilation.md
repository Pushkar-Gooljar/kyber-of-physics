# Annihilation 
>[!info] Definition: Annihilation
>- Annihilation occurs when a particle interacts with its antiparticle.
>- Mass-energy and momentum are conserved in the process.

![Annihilation of a positron and electron|400](https://s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/elements.cognitoedu.org/dd95be75-5f60-461c-b4e5-9239b43eb768/positron-electron-annihilation-diagram.png)

>[!concept] Positron and Electron Annihilation
>- The positron emitted by the decay of a Fluorine-18 nuclide travels a very short distance before it collides with an electron.
>- The positron and electron undergo **pair** annihilation.
>- The particles disappear, releasing their combined mass-energy as photons.
>- The mass of the electron and positron are converted into energy through the relation $E=\Delta mc^2$.
>-Assuming the electron and positron have negligible kinetic energy when they interact, the two photons appear with equal energies.
>- The photons have frequencies in the gamma ray range.
>- Assuming both particles were initially at rest before the collision, their initial momentum was negligible.
>- To conserve momentum, two photons must be emitted in opposite directions.

# Energy of Gamma Photons
>[!info] Before Annihilation
>- Positrons and Electrons have the same mass
>$$9.11 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$$
>- Their total mass is 
>$$1.822 \times 10^{-30}\text{ kg}$$

>[!info] After Annihilation
>- The positron and electron no longer exist.
>- Their combined mass is converted into photon energy.
>
>Using the relation $\Delta E=\Delta mc^2$:
>$$\Delta E = 1.822 \times 10^{-30} \times (3.0 \times 10^{8})^2$$
>$$\Delta E = 1.6398 \times 10^{-13}\text{ J} = 1024 \text{ keV}$$
>
>- Two photons are emitted in opposite directions in order to conserve momentum.
>- The photons have the same energy.
>  
>  The energy of one photon is therefore:
>$$\frac{1.6398 \times 10^{-13}}{2}=8.199 \times 10^{-14}\text{ J} = 512\text{ keV}$$

>[!concept] Frequency of Photon
>Using $E=hf$:
>$$f=\frac{E}{h}$$
>$$f=\frac{{8.199 \times 10^{-14}}}{6.63 \times 10^{-34}} = 1.24 \times 10^{20}\text{ Hz}$$

>[!concept] Momentum of Photon
>Using $E=pc$
>$$p=\frac{E}{c}$$
>$$f=\frac{{8.199 \times 10^{-14}}}{3.0 \times 10^8} = 2.73 \times 10^{-22}\text{ kg ms}^{-1}$$
