# Tracers
>[!info] Definition: Tracer
>- A substance containing radioactive nuclei that can be introduced into the body and is then absorbed by the tissue being studied.

## Types of Tracers
>[!info] PET Scanning
>For PET (Positron Emission Tomography) Scanning, the tracer used must be a $\beta^+$ emitter.

>[!info] Fluorine-18
>- Fluorine-18 is a radioactive nuclide which is a $\beta^+$ emitter.
>- It decays via the following equation
>$$\mathrm{^{18}_{9}F \to ^{18}_{8}O + ^{0}_{1}\beta^+ + ^0_{0}\nu} $$

>[!concept] Fluorine-18 Tracers
>- Fluorine-18 can bind to glucose molecules forming fluorodeoxyglucose.
>- The fluorodeoxyglucose has very similar chemical properties and structure to glucose, allowing it to be absorbed by the tissues to be studied during metabolic processes.
>- Fluorine-18 has a short half life ($\approx 110\text{ minutes}$), which minimises the exposure of the patient to ionising gamma rays.

# Usage of a Tracer
> [!concept] Usage of a Tracer
> 1. The tracer is introduced into the body via injection or ingestion.
> 2. The tracer travels through the body and is absorbed by specific tissues.
> 3. A radiation detector is used to track its progress.
> 4. Different radionuclides are used as the tracer depending on which substances are naturally absorbed by the tissue being studied.
