# Kinematics of SHM

## Displacement 
>[!math] Equation of Displacement with respect to Time
>$$\large x = x_{0}\sin \omega t$$
>
>**NOTE:** [[17.3 SHM from Circular Motion#Displacement|Derived using circular motion]]

## Velocity 
>[!math] Derivation of Velocity with respect to Time
>- Velocity $v$ is the rate of change of displacement, i.e. the derivative of displacement with respect to time:
>$$\large v = \frac{dx}{dt}$$
>- Since $x=x_{0}\sin \omega t$:
>$$\large\begin{align}v&=\frac{d}{dt}(x_{0}\sin \omega t)\\&=x_{0}\omega \cos \omega t\end{align}$$
>- Recall that linear velocity is given by $v=r\omega$, since in this case the radius is the amplitude, $v_{0}=x_{0}\omega$:
>$$\large v=v_{0}\cos \omega t$$

> [!math] Derivation of Velocity with respect to Displacement
> 
> Let the displacement $x$ and velocity $v$ of a simple harmonic oscillator be:
> 
> $$
> \begin{align}
> x &= x_{0}\sin(\omega t) \implies \sin(\omega t) = \frac{x}{x_{0}} \tag{1} \\
> v &= x_{0}\omega \cos(\omega t) \implies \cos(\omega t) = \frac{v}{x_{0}\omega} \tag{2}
> \end{align}
> $$
> 
> Using the Pythagorean trigonometric identity:
> 
> $$
> \sin^2(\omega t) + \cos^2(\omega t) = 1
> $$
> 
> Substitute equations $(1)$ and $(2)$ into the identity:
> 
> $$
> \left( \frac{x}{x_{0}} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{v}{x_{0}\omega} \right)^2 = 1
> $$
> 
> Expand the terms:
> 
> $$
> \frac{x^2}{x_{0}^2} + \frac{v^2}{x_{0}^2\omega^2} = 1
> $$
> 
> Multiply the entire equation by $x_{0}^2\omega^2$ to eliminate the denominators:
> 
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \omega^2 x^2 + v^2 &= x_{0}^2\omega^2 \\
> v^2 &= x_{0}^2\omega^2 - \omega^2 x^2 \\
> v^2 &= \omega^2(x_{0}^2 - x^2)
> \end{align}
> $$
> 
> Solve for $v$ by taking the square root of both sides:
> 
> $$
> \begin{align}
> v &= \pm \sqrt{\omega^2(x_{0}^2 - x^2)} \\
> v &= \pm \omega\sqrt{x_{0}^2 - x^2}
> \end{align}
> $$

## Acceleration 
>[!math] Derivation of Acceleration with respect to Time
>- Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, i.e. the derivative of velocity with respect to time:
>$$\large a = \frac{dv}{dt}$$
>- Since $v=v_{0}\cos \omega t$:
>$$\large \begin{align}\frac{dv}{dt} &= \frac{d}{dt}(v_{0}\cos \omega t) \\ &= -\omega v_{0}\sin \omega t \end{align}$$
>- Recall that linear velocity is given by $v=r\omega$, since in this case the radius is the amplitude, $v_{0}=x_{0}\omega$:
>$$\large \begin{align}a&=-\omega \times x_{0}\omega \times \sin \omega t \\ &= -\omega^2x_{0}\sin \omega t\end{align}$$
>- Since $x=x_{0}\sin \omega t$:
>$$\large a=-\omega^2x$$
