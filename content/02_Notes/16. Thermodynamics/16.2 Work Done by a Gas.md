>[!concept] Work Done by a Gas
>- When a gas **expands**, it **does work** on its surroundings by exerting pressure on the walls of its container.
> - Since work is done **by** the gas, its internal energy decreases.


![Piston Expansion|400](https://cdn.savemyexams.com/cdn-cgi/image/f=auto,width=3840/https://cdn.savemyexams.com/uploads/2021/04/16.1-Work-done-by-gas.png)

>[!math] Formula: Work Done at Constant Pressure
>1. Work done is defined as:
>$$
>\begin{align}
>\text{Work Done} &= \text{Force} \times \text{Distance moved in direction of force} \\
>W&=F\Delta s
>\end{align}
>$$
>2. Pressure is defined as:
>$$
>\begin{align}
>\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}} \\
>p=\frac{F}{A}
>\end{align}
>$$
>3. Rearranging to make force $F$ subject:
>$$
>F=pA
>$$
>4. Substituting into the expression for work:
>$$
>W=pA \times \Delta s
>$$
>5. Area times change in distance is equal to change in volume:
>$$
>W=p\Delta V
>$$

> [!warning] Examiner Tip: "External" Pressure
> The pressure $p$ in the equation $W = p\Delta V$ is the pressure of the **surroundings** (often atmospheric pressure), not necessarily the internal pressure of the gas itself. This is because when a gas expands, it is doing work *against* the external environment.

# Work Done on a Gas
>[!concept] Work Done on a Gas
>- Work is done on a gas when the gas is compressed.
>- Since work is done **on** the gas, its internal energy increases.
