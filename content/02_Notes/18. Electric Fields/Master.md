>[!info] Why electric potential near an isolated proton is positive (3/5)
>- Electric potential is defined as zero at infinity.
>- A unit positive test charge experiences a repulsive force from the proton
>- Work is done by an external force against this repulsive force to move the charge from infinity towards the proton

>[!tip] Why electric potential near an isolated electron is negative
>- Electric potential is defined as zero at infinity 
>- A unit positive test charge experiences an attractive force from the electron
>- Work is done by the attractive force as the charge moves from infinity towards the electron.

>[!info] Definition of electric field (1/2)
>- Force per unit positive charge

>[!info] Definition of electric potential at a point (2/3)
>- Work done per unit charge
>- in moving a positive test charge from infinity to the point

>[!info] Coulomb's Law (2/3)
>- The electric force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges
>- and is inversely proportional to the square of their separation 


>[!info] Why is electric field inside a conducting hollow sphere zero (1/3)
>- A charged particle placed inside experiences equal forces in all directions, so the resultant force and field is zero.

>[!info] Relationship between electric field and electric potential
>$$\text{Electric Field}=-\text{Potential Gradient}$$

>[!info] Why cant total electric potential and resultant electric field be simultaneously zero at point P

>- For total electric potential to be zero, X and Y must be oppositely charged.
> - For resultant electric field to be zero, the fields due X and Y at P must be in opposite directions, which requires the charges to have the same sign.
