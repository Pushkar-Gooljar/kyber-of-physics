# Tools
>[!info] Definition of Internal Energy
>$$\text{Internal Energy} = \sum{\text{Kinetic}}+\sum{\text{Potential}}$$

>[!info] First Law of Thermodynamics 
>$$\Delta U=w+q$$
>- $\Delta U$ = **Increase** in internal energy
>- $w$ = Work done **on** system
>- $q$ = Thermal energy supplied **to** system

>[!tip] Thermal Energy Transfer
>- If the transfer happens very quickly it is effectively negligible
>  
>- Thermal energy supplied > work done against atmospheric pressure
>  
>  ![thermal-energy-split|300](thermal-energy-split.svg)

>[!tip] Work Done
>- Work done on system to increase molecular spacing
>- Work done by system against atmospheric pressure
# Applications

**Use the first law of thermodynamics to explain why a bicycle pump gets hot when it is used to pump up a tyre quickly. [3]**
- Thermal energy transferred from system is negligible due to lack of time.
- Work is done on gas to compress it
- Internal energy increases so temperature increases

**With reference to molecular energies, explain why the temperature of water remains at 100 °C when it vaporises in a kettle, even though it is being heated. [3]**
- During vaporisation molecular spacing increases
- Thermal energy supplied is used by molecules to do work against intermolecular forces which increases potential energy.
- Kinetic energy of molecules is unchanged so temperature is unchanged

**Explain why, for a substance, the specific latent heat of vaporisation is usually greater than the specific latent heat of fusion. [3]**

- Vaporisation involves a greater change in volume
- More work is done against intermolecular forces to separate molecules
- Potential energy increases more while kinetic energy remains constant 
- So $\Delta U$ is greater 
- More work is done by molecules against atmospheric pressure 
- Since $q = \Delta U+\text{work done by system}$, more thermal energy must be supplied

**With reference to molecular kinetic and potential energies, describe and explain how the internal energy of the system changes when a gas is heated at constant volume so that its temperature increases. [3]**
- At constant volume molecular separation is constant so potential energy is constant 
- Since temperature increases kinetic energy of molecules increases
- Since Internal energy = sum of kinetic and potential energies of all molecules, internal energy increases

**With reference to molecular kinetic and potential energies, describe and explain how the internal energy of the system changes when a wire is stretched within its elastic limit at constant temperature. [3]**
- Since temperature is constant, kinetic energy is constant 
- Molecular spacing increases therefore potential energy increases 
- Since internal energy = sum of kinetic and potential energies of all molecules the internal energy increases


**Use the first law of thermodynamics to explain what happens to the internal energy of a sample of water when it evaporates from a rain puddle on a hot day. [3]**

The system is the evaporated water
- Thermal energy is transferred to the water
- Work is done by the water molecules against atmospheric pressure
- More thermal energy transferred than work done so internal energy increases
