## 1. Who gets targeted: the vulnerable

- ✅ **Facebook's "insecure teens" pitch (2017)**: a leaked internal document from Facebook Australia told advertisers it could identify when teenagers felt "insecure", "worthless" or "stressed". → *Digital advertising can target emotional vulnerability, not just demographics.*
- ✅ **Juul and youth vaping**: Juul's colourful, social-media-led launch marketing was blamed for a teen vaping surge; in 2022 it paid about **$438.5 million** to settle claims from over 30 US states that it marketed to young people. → *Targeting the young pays until regulators catch up.*
- ✅ **Tobacco history ("Joe Camel")**: the cartoon mascot was withdrawn in 1997 amid evidence it appealed to children. → *The industry has a long record of targeting the young.*
- ✅ **Gambling**: problem gamblers generate a disproportionate share of betting firms' profits; Italy banned almost all gambling advertising (2018), and Premier League clubs agreed to remove gambling sponsors from the front of shirts from the **2026–27** season. → *When harm is concentrated, advertising focuses on the people it harms most.*
- ✅ **Payday loans**: Google banned ads for high-interest, short-term loans in 2016. → *Platforms can choose not to profit from desperation.*
- ✅ **Cambridge Analytica (revealed 2018)**: data harvested from up to about **87 million** Facebook users was used for political micro-targeting. → *Targeting techniques extend from products to votes.*
- ✅ **Loot boxes**: Belgium effectively banned paid loot boxes in video games in 2018 as gambling aimed at children.

## 2. Regulation: can advertising be made honest and fair?

- ✅ **Quebec (since 1980)** bans commercial advertising aimed at children under 13; **Sweden (since 1991)** bans TV ads aimed at under-12s. → *Protecting children through regulation is decades old and workable.*
- ✅ **EU Digital Services Act (in force 2023–24)**: bans ads targeted at minors based on profiling, and ads targeted using sensitive data such as religion or sexuality. → *Regulation is moving from what ads say to how they are targeted.*
- ✅ **Tobacco**: most countries have comprehensive ad bans under the WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control; **Australia introduced plain packaging in 2012**, the first country to do so. → *The strongest evidence that restricting advertising changes behaviour.*
- ⚠️ **UK junk-food ad restrictions**: a ban on ads for foods high in fat, sugar or salt on TV before 9pm and in paid online ads; the legal start was delayed to January 2026 (with a voluntary start in October 2025). Verify the date.
- ✅ **France's influencer law (2023)**: influencers must label paid and edited content, and cannot promote cosmetic surgery or some risky financial products. → *Law is catching up with influencer marketing.*
- ✅ **UK Advertising Standards Authority**: a mostly self-regulatory body that bans misleading ads after complaints. → *Useful for "stricter regulation is the only way"*: self-regulation also works, but only after the ad has run.

## 3. Truth and deception

- ✅ **Greenwashing**: in 2024 a Dutch court ruled that KLM's "Fly Responsibly" ads were misleading; the EU will ban vague green claims such as "eco-friendly" without proof from **2026**. → *Environmental claims are the new frontier of misleading advertising.*
- ✅ **Volkswagen "Clean Diesel" (2015)**: ads promoted clean cars while the engines used software to cheat emissions tests. → *An extreme case of advertising built on a lie.*
- ✅ **Tobacco's "doctors recommend" era (1930s–50s)**: cigarette ads used doctors to claim health benefits. → *A historical baseline showing how far regulation has come.*
- Mark-scheme idea: *most advertisements do not lie; they persuade more subtly, by association and emotion.* → The best **antithesis** to "advertising rarely tells the truth".

## 4. Influence and power: is advertising losing its grip?

- ⚠️ **Global advertising spending passed $1 trillion a year in 2024** (WPP Media/GroupM), and about **three-quarters** is now digital. → *Advertising is growing, not declining.*
- ⚠️ **Google and Meta** earn most of their revenue from advertising (Meta about **97%**) and together take a large share of global digital ad spending. → *Advertising funds the internet, and a few companies control it.*
- ✅ **Ad blocking**: hundreds of millions of people use ad blockers, and advertisers have moved to influencers and product placement. → *Advertising is changing form, not losing influence.*
- ✅ **Influencer marketing**: brands pay creators to promote products as if they were personal recommendations. → *The line between advertising and content has blurred.*
- Mark-scheme ideas: *the age-old techniques and targets of advertising remain unchanged*; *many long-standing campaigns stay successful regardless of digital technology.*

## 5. The case for advertising

- ✅ **It funds free content**: most free websites, apps, search engines and a large share of news are paid for by advertising. → *Without ads, much of the internet would sit behind paywalls, hurting poorer users most.*
- ⚠️ **Public-health campaigns**: the US "truth" anti-smoking campaign (from 2000) was credited with preventing hundreds of thousands of young people from starting to smoke; the UK's "Stoptober" encourages quitting each October. → *Advertising techniques can serve public good.*
- ✅ **Information for small businesses**: targeted online ads let small firms reach customers cheaply, where TV advertising was once affordable only for big brands.
- ✅ **Advertising as art**: Guinness "Surfer" (1999) and Apple's "1984" (directed by Ridley Scott) are regularly ranked among the best adverts ever made; Andy Warhol turned advertising imagery into pop art. → *For "to what extent is advertising a form of art?"*
- ✅ **Dove "Real Beauty" (from 2004)**: used ordinary women instead of models, and was praised for challenging beauty norms. → *Advertising can shape social attitudes positively*, though it still exists to sell soap.
