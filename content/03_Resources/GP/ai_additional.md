## Advantages of AI in Education

1. Personalised learning
AI can adapt lessons to each student's ability.
Students can learn at their own pace.
Weak areas can be identified quickly.

2. Affordable tutoring
Private tutoring can be expensive.
AI tutors can provide assistance at any time.
This could make additional academic support more accessible.

3. Instant feedback
Students do not always have to wait for a teacher to mark their work.
AI can identify errors and suggest improvements immediately.

4. Reduces teachers' workload
AI can help create quizzes, lesson plans and worksheets.
It can assist with repetitive administrative work.
Teachers can therefore spend more time interacting with students.

5. Greater accessibility
AI can translate information.
It can support students with different learning needs.
It can make educational material available outside normal school hours.

6. Preparation for the future
AI skills are increasingly important in the workplace.
Teaching students to use AI responsibly can improve their digital literacy.

7. More engaging classrooms
Smart classrooms can use interactive tools, simulations and AI-powered learning activities. This may make lessons more engaging for some students.

## Disadvantages of AI in Education

1. Cheating
Students may use AI to complete assignments instead of learning.
This creates problems for academic integrity.

2. Loss of critical thinking
If students constantly ask AI for answers, they may practise independent thinking less.
Education could become focused on obtaining answers rather than understanding ideas.

3. Inaccurate information
AI can produce false or misleading information.
Students may accept answers without checking reliable sources.

4. Privacy concerns
AI educational platforms can process large amounts of student information.
This raises questions about how personal data is collected and protected. UNESCO specifically highlights data privacy as an important issue.

5. Digital divide
Wealthier schools may have access to advanced AI tools.
Poorer schools may lack devices, internet access or trained teachers.
Therefore, AI could reduce inequality or worsen it, depending on how it is implemented.

6. Reduced human interaction
A teacher provides encouragement, empathy and emotional support.
AI cannot fully reproduce genuine human relationships.

7. Dependence on technology
Excessive reliance on AI could weaken students' ability to research, write and solve problems independently.

## Evaluation

The strongest GP argument is not simply "AI is good" or "AI is bad."

A better argument is: AI should be viewed as a tool that enhances education rather than a replacement for teachers.

Schools should:
- teach students how to use AI responsibly;
- establish clear rules about acceptable and unacceptable use;
- protect students' personal data;
- train teachers;
- continue using human interaction;
- adapt assessments to test genuine understanding.

UNESCO similarly promotes a human-centred approach, arguing that AI should be used safely, ethically and meaningfully.

## GP Essay Question

**"Artificial intelligence will improve education more than it will damage it." Discuss.**

### Introduction
Artificial intelligence has rapidly entered the educational environment, transforming how students learn and how teachers deliver lessons. AI-powered tutors can provide personalised assistance, while smart classrooms can use technology to make learning more interactive and efficient. However, the rapid adoption of AI has also created concerns about cheating, inaccurate information, privacy and the possible decline of independent thinking. Although AI presents significant risks, its educational benefits can outweigh its disadvantages if it is introduced responsibly and remains under human supervision.

### Paragraph 1 — AI can personalise education
One major advantage of artificial intelligence is its ability to personalise learning. Traditional classrooms often require one teacher to address the needs of many students at the same time, making it difficult to provide individual attention. AI tutors can instead analyse a student's performance and provide exercises suited to their particular weaknesses. This could be especially beneficial to students who struggle academically or cannot afford private tuition. Sal Khan has argued that AI could potentially provide every student with a personal tutor. Therefore, AI could make high-quality academic support more accessible.

### Paragraph 2 — AI can support teachers
Furthermore, AI can improve education by reducing teachers' administrative workload. It can assist with preparing lesson plans, generating quizzes and providing initial feedback on students' work. This allows teachers to dedicate more time to mentoring students and explaining difficult concepts. Bill Gates argues that AI should not replace teachers but should "support and empower them." Consequently, the greatest benefit may come from cooperation between teachers and AI rather than from replacing human educators.

### Paragraph 3 — However, AI can encourage cheating
Nevertheless, the use of AI presents a serious threat to academic integrity. Students can potentially use generative AI to produce essays, answer questions or complete assignments without developing the necessary skills themselves. This makes it more difficult for teachers to determine whether submitted work genuinely reflects a student's abilities. The problem is already widespread: Pew Research Center found that 26% of US teenagers had used ChatGPT for schoolwork in 2024, double the proportion recorded in 2023. Therefore, schools need clear rules that distinguish legitimate assistance from dishonest use.

### Paragraph 4 — AI could weaken independent thinking
Another concern is that excessive dependence on AI could undermine critical thinking. If students immediately ask an AI system to solve every problem, they may have fewer opportunities to struggle with difficult questions, research independently and develop their own arguments. Education should not merely provide students with answers; it should teach them how to analyse evidence and form judgements. AI can therefore become harmful when it acts as a shortcut rather than a learning tool.

### Paragraph 5 — AI can also create inequality
AI may also deepen existing educational inequalities. Wealthier schools are more likely to possess advanced digital infrastructure and trained staff, whereas poorer institutions may struggle to provide reliable internet access and appropriate devices. Furthermore, UNESCO found that fewer than 10% of surveyed schools and universities had formal guidance concerning generative AI in 2023. This suggests that technological development has been faster than the development of appropriate educational policies.

### Conclusion
In conclusion, artificial intelligence has the potential to improve education significantly through personalized learning, instant feedback, affordable tutoring and support for teachers. Nevertheless, its benefits are not automatic. Unregulated AI can encourage cheating, weaken independent thinking, threaten privacy and widen inequalities. Therefore, AI should neither be blindly embraced nor completely rejected. The most effective approach is a human-centred education system in which AI enhances the work of teachers while students remain responsible for their own learning.
