# Education and AI: evidence bank

Every named example and statistic from your Education (EDU) and ICT/AI (ICT) notes, grouped by the argument it serves.
**Rank** = versatility rank out of all 100+ items (from the 227-question planning run); lower is more useful. **⚠️** = a figure in your notes is wrong or unverified, so learn the **safe version** instead.

Format: **Item (year)**: fact. → *what it proves*. `FOR` / `AGAINST` / `BOTH` = direction for a pro-technology or pro-education claim.

## 1. AI and technology in learning

|---|---|
| 12 | **China's remote "dual-teacher" lessons (EDU-N002 / ICT-N006)**: lessons by top urban teachers broadcast to rural schools, reaching tens of millions of students; research found gains in results and later earnings. ⚠️ The "32%" and "38%" figures are unverified, so don't quote them. → *Technology spreads good teaching when a local teacher stays in the room.* `FOR` (its condition makes it a strong **synthesis** example) |
| 25 | **Sweden goes back to books (EDU-N009, 2023–)**: after PIRLS reading scores fell from **555 to 544**, Sweden funded printed textbooks and cut screens in early years. ⚠️ Check the "2.1 billion SEK" amount. → *Digital learning is not automatically better.* `AGAINST` |

| 45 | **Human Rights Watch ed-tech report (ICT-N021 / ICT-S006)**: **89% of 163** government-recommended pandemic ed-tech products surveilled or could surveil children. ⚠️ Published **2022**, not 2023. → *Children's data is the hidden price of "free" learning tools.* `AGAINST` |

### From your AI-in-learning essay (not in your notes; checked, but verify the exact numbers)

- **Harvard AI physics tutor (Kestin et al., 2024–25)**: students taught by an AI tutor built to guide step by step learned **more than twice as much** as peers in active-learning lectures, in less time. → *AI helps when it keeps the thinking with the student.* `FOR`
- **Turkish high-school maths study (Bastani et al., 2024)**: unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores by **48%**, but exam scores without AI fell by **17%**; a tutor version with safeguards largely avoided the drop. → *AI as a substitute harms learning.* `AGAINST`
- **MIT Media Lab "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (2025)**: students writing essays with ChatGPT showed weaker brain connectivity and poor recall of their own sentences. Small sample. → *Cognitive skills weaken when they aren't used.* `AGAINST`
- **Khanmigo (Khan Academy)**: an AI tutor that refuses to give answers and asks Socratic questions instead. → *Guardrails can be designed in.* `FOR`
- **Estonia's AI Leap (2025)**: AI tools for secondary students together with teacher training. → *States can shape how AI is used in schools.* `FOR`

## 2. Access and inequality

| Rank | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | **ITU digital divide (ICT-S001 / ICT-N016, 2025)**: about **74%** of the world online (about 6 billion), but only about a quarter in low-income countries; **2.2 billion** offline, and growth is slowing. → *Technology's benefits are unequally distributed.* The usual **antithesis** to any tech-optimist claim. |
| 2 | **M-Pesa, Kenya (ICT-N004 / ICT-S003)**: ⚠️ the "23.6% in 2021" baseline is wrong. **Safe version: a large majority of Kenyan adults use mobile money; M-Pesa brought banking to people without bank accounts.** → *Technology can make up for missing institutions.* `FOR` |
| 14 | **UN SDG 4 (EDU-N003 / EDU-S003, 2024)**: **273 million** children and young people out of school; **754 million** adults illiterate, about two-thirds women. → *Access, not quality, is still the first barrier.* `BOTH` |
| 31 | **OECD completion gap (EDU-S005 / EDU-N014, 2025)**: **70%** of young adults with university-educated parents complete tertiary education vs **26%** of those whose parents lack upper-secondary education. → *Family background shapes outcomes.* `AGAINST` a "meritocracy" claim |
| 139 | **UNESCO teacher shortage (EDU-N004 / EDU-S002, 2024)**: **44 million** more teachers needed by 2030, **15 million** of them in sub-Saharan Africa. → *AI cannot replace teachers that don't exist; it may have to fill some of the gap.* `BOTH` |
| n/a | **Common Sense Media (ICT-N015 / ICT-S005, 2025)**: **51%** of children aged 0–8 have their own device or personal access to one. → *Screens start before school does.* |

## 5. Regulating AI and platforms

| Rank | Item |
|---|---|
| 9 | **EU AI Act (ICT-N001 / ICT-S009, 2024)**: the first comprehensive AI law; risk tiers; "unacceptable" uses banned from **February 2025**; fines up to **€35 million or 7%** of global turnover. → *Democracies can regulate before harm occurs.* `FOR` |
| 177 | **AI Act delay (ICT-N017 / ICT-N033, 2026)**: obligations for high-risk systems pushed from August 2026 to December 2027. → *Regulation lags behind the technology.* `AGAINST` |
| 8 | **The deepfake gap (ICT-N020)**: ⚠️ drop the "300% surge". **Safe version: open-weight AI models make deepfakes easy to create, and enforcement lags despite new laws.** `AGAINST` regulation working |
| 13 | **Australia's under-16 social media ban (ICT-N003 / ICT-N032 / ICT-S010)**: law passed 2024, in force **December 2025**; fines up to **A$49.5 million**. The weak point: the age-check trial wrongly estimated about **8.5%** of 16-year-olds (STA-S012). `BOTH` |
| 15 | **Brazil v X (ICT-N002 / ICT-N018 / ICT-S013, 2024)**: the Supreme Court suspended X and froze Starlink/X funds; X briefly bypassed the block using Cloudflare, then paid the fines (about **$5 million**) and appointed a local representative. → *Technology can dodge the state at first, but states can win in the end.* `BOTH` |
| 35 | **COMPAS algorithm (ICT-N010)**: ⚠️ ProPublica's investigation dates from **2016**. The algorithm wrongly labelled Black defendants high-risk nearly twice as often as white defendants. → *AI can automate bias.* `AGAINST` |
| 71 / 112 | **Gig work (ICT-N012 / ICT-N007 / ICT-S011)**: the EU Platform Work Directive (2024) presumes gig workers are employees and requires a human to review automated account suspensions; member states must adopt it by **December 2026**. The EU has about 28 million platform workers. → *Algorithmic management needs human accountability.* `BOTH` |
| 186 | **Dispute: open vs closed AI models (ICT-N036)**: safety risk vs monopoly power. `BOTH` |
