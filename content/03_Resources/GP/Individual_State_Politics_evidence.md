# The individual, the state and political systems: evidence bank

Every named example and statistic from your **Individual and Society (IND)**, **The State (STA)** and **Politics and Government (POL)** notes, grouped by the argument it serves. This covers leadership, institutions, citizens and power.

**Rank** = versatility rank across all items in the 227-question planning run (lower is more useful; "–" = in your notes but not used in the plans). **⚠️** = a figure in your notes is wrong or unverified, so learn the **safe version**.

Format: **Item (year)**: fact → *what it proves*.

## 1. Citizens against the state: protest and participation

| Rank | Item |
|---|---|
| 4 | **Kenya Gen Z protests (IND-N007, June 2024)**: leaderless youth protests organised online forced President Ruto to withdraw the Finance Bill. → *Digital networks let citizens hold power to account without parties or leaders.* |
| 89 / 88 | **Why the bill existed (STA-N002 / STA-S008)**: the bill aimed to raise about **$2.7 billion** in taxes, under IMF pressure, while debt repayments consumed around half of government revenue. → *The state's choices were constrained by creditors, so it is not simply a villain.* Use it as the antithesis to "governments serve the wealthy". |
| 37 | **Bangladesh July Uprising (IND-N001, 2024)**: student protests against civil-service job quotas grew into a mass movement that forced Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina to resign and flee. → *Young people can topple entrenched leaders.* |
| 48 | **South Korea: martial law reversed (IND-N008, December 2024)**: within hours, **190** lawmakers pushed into the blockaded National Assembly and voted unanimously to lift martial law while citizens gathered outside. → *Citizens and legislators together can stop a power grab.* |
| 91 | **France pension reform suspended (IND-N014, 2025)**: the National Assembly paused the 2023 rise in the retirement age to 64 after years of mass protest. → *Sustained public pressure can reverse unpopular policy.* |

| 222 | **Athenian assembly (POL-N018, 5th century BCE)**: direct democracy with quorums of 6,000 citizens, plus ostracism to exile over-powerful figures. → *Direct democracy is old, but its "citizens" excluded women, slaves and foreigners.* |

## 8. Leadership: a quick map

- **Power corrupts / emergency powers stick:** Caesar, dictator for life · South Korea's martial law · US executive overreach · Georgia · Taiwan's court.
- **Power checked:** South Korea's court (8–0) · Brazil's Supreme Court · Botswana · Germany's Article 67.
- **Leaders removed by the people:** Hasina (Bangladesh) · Ruto's bill (Kenya) · the ANC losing its majority (South Africa).
- **Effective without democracy:** Singapore · Rwanda · China's railways. The antithesis: Lebanon and Taiwan show how quickly institutions decay when power is unchecked.

## 9. Pairs to learn together

- **Singapore ↔ Kenya Gen Z**: effective government without participation vs participation as accountability
- **South Korea (190 lawmakers) ↔ Caesar, dictator for life**: power checked vs emergency power made permanent
- **Germany's Article 67 ↔ France's 2024 collapse**: constitutional design and stability
- **Mexico's elected judges ↔ South Korea's Constitutional Court**: "more democracy" vs independent guardrails
- **Carbon Majors (57 producers) ↔ individual action**: structure vs the individual
- **31% feel they have a say ↔ 69% vs 22% trust gap**: voice builds trust
- **Kenya's protests ↔ Kenya's debt**: citizens vs creditors, not just citizens vs the state
