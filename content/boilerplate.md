# <u>Defining The Problem</u>
- Independent variable: $a$
- Dependent variable: $T$
- Quantities to be kept constant:  Distance $L$, Same bar used to keep $M$ constant

# <u>Method of Analysis</u>
A graph of $\ln T$ against $\ln a$ is plotted. If the suggested relationship is valid a straight line not passing through the origin is expected.

![ㅤ|400](exm.png)

# <u>Preliminary Steps</u>
- A digital top-pan balance is used to measure $M$
- Two identical string loops are used to suspend the bar from the rod and a spirit level is used to ensure the bar is levelled.
- A half metre rule is used to measure $L$ on two sides of the bar and an average is calculated.
- A set square is placed against the half-metre rule and bar to ensure they are perpendicular.
- The length of the bar is measured using a metre rule and a mark is made at its centre.

# <u>Procedure</u>
- Using a metre rule, the loops of both strings are set a distance of $a$ m from the centre of the bar and adhesive putty is used to fix them in place.
- The loop of strings on the rod are adjusted until the strings are parallel. A set square is used to check if they are perpendicular to the bar and rod. 
- The end of the bar is displaced horizontally and slow motion video camera is started. 
- At time $t_{1}$ the bar is released. After 20 oscillations, the time $t_{2}$ is measured by viewing the recording frame b frame. 
- $T$ is calculated via $\frac{{t_{2}-t_{1}}}{20}$. For the same value of $a$ the experiment is repeated twice and an average value of $T$ is calculated.
# <u>Safety Precautions</u>
- A sand tray is placed below the rod to prevent it from rolling off the bench and hurting the experimenter if it slips from the string.
