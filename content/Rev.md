### 📚 Revision and Study

**Q: How did you make use of mark schemes and examiner reports for revision/study?**
**A:** I mainly availed myself of ERs [Examiner Reports] for MCQs for Chemistry, Physics and Biology. Often, the crux of the question and different methods of obtaining the correct answer are outlined. ERs and MS [Mark Schemes] are crucial for Physics P5: remember the specific apparatus (the jargon), the order in which the ms is laid out and how the marks are allocated for additional details. They are equally important for Question 2 about calculating errors and drawing LBF [Line of Best Fit] and LWF [Line of Worst Fit] properly.

**Q: What study technique(s) did you use? Did you use flashcards, blurting, or mostly just re-doing past paper questions?**
**A:** I did not use any specific named revision technique. I used neither flashcards nor blurting. My main asset: active recall. Usually, before attending tuition, I would just revise what was covered the week prior for around 10 min.

**Q: How did you plan your study/revision?**
**A:** I did not have any revision plan or timeline. Everything was impromptu. I had a Notes document on my phone for every question that I had found unique and rare; most of which were encountered in Lower 6. Ahead of mock exams and final exams, I would just look up the year and work out that question.

**Q: Did you have a specific study routine like a specific time period where you would be more productive?**
**A:** Being productive for most of a day is unachievable. I experimented with 10 min revision sessions at 6 in the morning for tuition that I had later on week days. That was not efficient for me. I ended up switching to glancing the notes for 10 min at night before bed.

**Q: What did you do extra, apart from school and tuition for revision and study?**
**A:** The term “extra/personal work” is nebulous and misunderstood. My extra work was about watching videos on the concepts and gathering data on the unique questions.

**Q: Do you have any specific tips to aid memorisation of definitions or lists of ideas? / Did you have a specific method for extracting and memorizing keywords?**
**A:** Memorizing keywords will not be an issue ever with regular practice. Make sure to include specific words outlined in the syllabus pdf.

### ✍️ Exam Technique

**Q: How did you deal with questions you had no idea how to answer (if any)?**
**A:** For questions that I had no idea, my mindset would be that “I have done the chapter, there must be some concept to be applied”. As for “suggest…” questions that carry 1 or 2 marks, placed at the end of the huge question, the answer lies in the previous parts of the question. For Physics, it could from a previous calculation leading to a weird value or a correct value.

**Q: How did you structure your answers for physics and chemistry (especially 3 or 4 marks ones)?**
**A:** To answer ANY structured question, I would always start underlining the keywords and recasting or writing whatever the word means to me. Then, I would plan out the question in note form with a pencil on the side and try to see if I have included everything I know on the concept (scatter gun approach) and crucially, the answer is relevant.

**Q: What technique/method did you use when practicing doing past papers timed?**
**A:** When practising past papers, I would just set a timer. If the paper is 1h15min, I would choose 45min. For the first 2 minutes, I would scan the paper, writing any definitions or answers to questions I had already encountered with a pencil.

**Q: What did you do in situations where you were unsure about two possible answers (e.g. MCQ options or concepts for structured)?**
**A:** Whenever I be stuck in a situation where 2 answers seem plausible, I would often try to find the catch in one of them. That is pretty daunting, especially for Bio MCQ – I failed most of the time and ended up Googling the question/glance at ERs.
