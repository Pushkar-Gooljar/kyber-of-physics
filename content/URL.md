# Defining Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

## The Definition (LO 2)

Simple Harmonic Motion is a specific type of oscillation defined by the relationship between acceleration and displacement.

> [!quote] The Golden Rule of SHM
> SHM occurs when a body's acceleration is **directly proportional** to its displacement from a fixed point (equilibrium) and is directed **towards that fixed point**.

Mathematically:
$$
a \propto -x
$$

## The Equation (LO 3)

Replacing the proportionality with the constant ($$\omega^2$$):

$$
a = -\omega^2 x
$$

### Deconstructing the Equation
1.  **The Minus Sign:** This is crucial. It indicates that the acceleration (and therefore the restoring force) is always in the **opposite direction** to the displacement. If the body moves right ($$+x$$), the force pulls left.
2.  **$$\omega^2$$:** The constant of proportionality is the square of the angular frequency. Since $$\omega^2$$ is always positive, the minus sign ensures the direction is always opposite.

> [!danger] Common Pitfall
> If asked to "State the conditions for SHM" in an exam:
> 1.  Do not just write the equation. You must describe it in words.
> 2.  Do not forget to mention the **direction** (opposite to displacement or towards equilibrium). This is usually the second mark.

### Intuition: The Restoring Force
From Newton's Second Law ($$F=ma$$):
$$
F = m(-\omega^2 x) = -(m\omega^2)x
$$
This looks like Hooke's Law ($$F = -kx$$), where $$k = m\omega^2$$. This is why springs are the classic example of SHM.
