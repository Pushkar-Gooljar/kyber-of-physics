> [!summary] Core Geopolitical Premise
> As global temperatures rise, glaciers melt, and populations expand, transboundary fresh water has shifted from a shared natural resource to a highly contested strategic asset. **Hydro-hegemony**—the domination of a river basin's water resources by an upstream state through dam construction—has become a primary driver of diplomatic friction, proxy conflict, and potential kinetic war in the 21st century.

# Key Concepts
>[!info] Hydro-Hegemony
>Control over a shared water resource achieved through geopolitical power, infrastructure development, and the imposition of favourable legal frameworks.

>[!info] Riparian States
>- Sovereign nations through which a reiver flows.
>- Categorised into:
>	- **Upstream States:** Positioned near the headwaters, giving them physical control over water flow. (e.g. Ethiopia, Turkey, China, India)
>	- **Downstream States:** Positioned near the river's outlet, making them highly vulnerable to upstream alterations (e.g. Egypt, Iraq, Vietnam, Pakistan)

>[!info] Physical vs Economic Water Scarcity
>- **Physical Scarcity:** Demand exceeds the actual available physical supply of water.
>- **Economic Scarcity:**  Water exists, but poor management, lack of infrastructure, or institutional weaknesses prevent access to clean water.

# Case Study: The Blue Nile Basin and GERD
>[!abstract] Key Points
>- **Upstream:** Ethiopia constructs GERD. Fills reservoirs (2020-2024), fully activating turbines by September 2025.
>- **Midstream:** Sudan faces variable agricultural impacts and up to 25% seepage water loss into the fractured Nubian block.
>- **Downstream** Egypt faces altered streamflow, with a projected 12% to 25% flow reduction during filling, triggering an urgent water security crisis and intense diplomatic friction.
