
# UNSC Paralysis and Selective International Law

> [!info] Post-Cold War Structural Decay
> The twenty-first-century geopolitical landscape is undergoing a structural transition characterized by the erosion of post-Cold War security frameworks and the emergence of a highly competitive, multipolar global order.

> [!danger] Systemic Consequences
> - **Global Rearmament:** Unprecedented global military expenditure reached **$2,887 billion ($2.89 trillion) in 2025**—marking the eleventh consecutive year of positive growth.
> - **Historic Displacement:** By the end of 2024, forcibly displaced populations exceeded **123 million individuals (123.2 million, or 1 in every 67 people on Earth)**.

---

## Structural Paralysis of the UN Security Council

> [!info] The "League of Nations" Parallel
> Just as the League of Nations collapsed in the 1930s due to its inability to stop aggressive actions by major powers (Japan in Manchuria, Italy in Abyssinia), the UNSC is increasingly seen as impotent. This institutional decay forces states into unilateral rearmament, such as Germany increasing its defense budget by **24% year-on-year to $114 billion in 2025**.

> [!info] Structural Flaw: The P5 Veto
> The United Nations Security Council was designed to maintain global order, yet its five permanent members (US, UK, France, Russia, China) hold individual veto power under Article 27.

> [!danger] Systemic Exploitation of Veto Power
> - **Russian Federation:** Consistently used its veto to block resolutions condemning its invasion of Ukraine. This has forced Ukraine to sustain a wartime military burden of **$84.1 billion (40% of its GDP) in 2025**, heavily reliant on external aid (€72.3B from EU, €64.3B from US).
> - **United States:** Frequently used its veto to shield Israel, blocking resolutions calling for immediate ceasefires in the Gaza humanitarian crisis, as well as resolutions challenging settlement expansions.
> - **China & Russia:** Jointly utilize vetoes to block humanitarian interventions and sanctions in regions of strategic interest (e.g., Syria, Myanmar, North Korea).

## Selective Application of International Law

> [!info] Double Standards
> Global South nations perceive international law not as a universal moral imperative, but as a flexible tool enforced selectively by Western powers to preserve hegemony.

> [!danger] Double Standards Matrix
> | Area of Evaluation | Western Stance A (Russian Aggression in Ukraine) | Western Stance B (Gaza Humanitarian Crisis) |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | **Sovereignty & Territorial Integrity** | Enforced as a universal, non-negotiable rules-based order. Unilateral annexations are declared illegal and invalid. | Applied selectively. Annexation of the Golan Heights and systemic settlement expansions in the West Bank are met with diplomatic tolerance. |
> | **Economic Sanctions** | Immediate, sweeping economic & political isolation ($300B assets frozen, SWIFT ban). | Continued financial, military, and arms support despite documented violations of civilian infrastructure protection. |
> | **Legal Action & ICC** | Swift support for ICC arrest warrants against Vladimir Putin (2023) and ICJ genocide backing. | Active pushback, condemnation, and threats of sanctions against ICC officials seeking arrest warrants for Israeli leaders. |

## Geopolitical Repercussions: The Global South's Pivot

> [!info] Rejection of Unilateral Sanctions
> Key Global South nations (India, Brazil, South Africa, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia) refuse to participate in unilateral Western sanctions, viewing them as selective.
> - *Example:* India expanded its trade with Russia, lowering Russia's share of its arms imports from 51% to 40% between 2021 and 2025, prioritizing its own energy security and civilian needs.

> [!abstract] Institutional Alternatives
> The paralysis of Western-dominated institutions has accelerated the expansion of alternative geopolitical bodies like **BRICS+** and the **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)**, creating parallel economic and diplomatic networks.

> [!example] South Africa's ICJ Challenge
> South Africa bypassed Western diplomatic paralysis by initiating a landmark genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice (ICJ) under the 1948 Genocide Convention, a move widely supported across the Global South to challenge selective legal enforcement.

## Systematic Humanitarian Failures

> [!danger] Unchecked Asymmetric Humanitarian Disasters
> - **Yemen:** The civil war and subsequent blockades resulted in **377,000 deaths**, with **59% (223,000) caused by indirect factors** (hunger, disease, and public health collapse), illustrating how modern asymmetric conflicts disproportionately harm vulnerable civilian populations.
> - **Sudan:** The conflict became the world's largest forced displacement crisis by late 2024, uprooting **14.3 million Sudanese** (nearly 1 in 3 citizens) while the international community remained diplomatically paralyzed.

> [!info] The Host Burden
> Contrary to political narratives in wealthier Western nations, low- and middle-income countries host **73% of the world's refugees**, with **67% of displaced persons** remaining in countries neighboring their nation of origin.
